The business

A five-star store with a two-star website

A family furniture store on Plaza Saint-Hubert — Montréal's covered-marquee shopping strip. They sell sectionals, mattresses, bedroom sets and dining sets between $46 and $5,400, with a median ticket around $800. They deliver free across Montréal and Laval, carry it inside, assemble it, and finance it. People who buy from them rate them 5.0. People who find them online mostly don't buy at all.

What they actually sell

Not furniture — a finished room, this week, without a truck rental or a friend with a dolly. 44% of the catalog is over $800, which is exactly the band where delivery, assembly and financing decide the sale, not the price tag.

Who buys

Montréal households furnishing a 4½ or a first condo, and families replacing a set. Budget-aware but not cheap — they'll spend $2,400 on a sectional if it can be $67 a month. They search in French and in English, and half of them are on a phone.

Who they're up against

Brault & Martineau and Meubles RD on flyers and financing. Mobilia on taste. Wayfair and Amazon on selection. None of them are fifteen minutes away with the sofa already in the building. That's the whole opening.

The idea

Stop selling discounts. Start selling certainty.

The current site competes on the one axis where a 171-product store can never win: price theatre. Amazon is cheaper, Wayfair has more, and Brault & Martineau's flyer is louder. So the new site competes somewhere else entirely — on the three questions that actually stop a Montréal furniture purchase, which a local store with a warehouse and a truck is uniquely able to answer.

MECHANIC 01

Ça rentre-tu ?
The Montréal fit check

Montréal is a city of spiral staircases, outdoor triplex stairs, 30-inch doors and 4½s. The universal fear before buying a sectional here is not the price — it's whether it physically gets into the apartment. Every large product gets a three-tap check: entrance type, door width, floor. It answers in plain language.

  • Green — “Ça rentre. Livré monté.”
  • Amber — “Serré. On le démonte en 3 pièces, la plus large fait 28 po.”
  • Red — “Ne rentre pas — voici 3 sectionnels qui rentrent chez vous.”

The data already exists in their own product copy. It just has to move out of prose and into fields. No national retailer will ever build this, because it only makes sense for one city.

MECHANIC 02 · HELD BACK

En stock à Montréal
Certainty now, a date later

Wayfair says three to five weeks. Big-box says “in stock”. A local store with the sofa in the building could say a weekday — and the built site can, but does not. Naming a day requires inventory that is true at the SKU level and a delivery calendar the site can read. Neither exists yet, and a weekday the truck misses costs more trust than the vaguer line ever wins.

  • Shipping today → En stock à Montréal, on the card and the product page
  • No stock → “Sur commande · 2 à 3 semaines”, honestly
  • Always → gratuit · monté · à l'étage

The date mechanic is built and wired end to end behind one flag — SHOW_DELIVERY_DATES. Turn it on the day stock and the delivery calendar are trustworthy; nothing else has to change. Until then the strongest true line is the one that beats a three-week wait anyway: it is already in the building.

MECHANIC 03

67 $ / mois
The price people actually shop

Median ticket $800; sectionals $2,400–$4,000. In this market that purchase is made in monthly payments — it's how Brault & Martineau and Meubles RD sell, and this store already offers financing. Today the offer dead-ends in a link to nowhere.

  • Monthly figure under every price, on every card
  • A real financing page: rates, terms, what you need to apply
  • Pre-approval before the cart, so the sofa is already affordable
  • Klarna and Afterpay — 4 payments, for the smaller basket
  • Affirm — monthly, where a sectional actually lands
  • In-store financing — the existing offer, finally given a page

Eligibility is computed from the price and recomputed on every variant change, because pay-in-4 caps out below the price of a sectional and showing it anyway is a promise checkout would break. Lowest effort of the three mechanics, and on a catalog with 44% of products above $800, likely the fastest revenue.

Supporting: the store as proof

79 five-star reviews, quoted with names, on the homepage and on every product page. Real photography of the showroom, the delivery truck and the people who carry it up your stairs. A showroom page with hours, a map, parking, and a “Réservez un essai — 20 min” booking that turns an online browse into a visit.

Supporting: complete the room

They sell sets, but the site sells pieces. On every bed: “Complétez la chambre” — add the nightstands, dresser and mirror, and watch the bundle price drop as you go. On every sectional: the coffee table and rug from the same photo. Direct lift on average order value at zero media cost.

Supporting: bilingual, properly

Two real languages with hreflang, not a translate widget — /fr/ and /en/, both indexed, both with native product titles and descriptions. The English half of Montréal's furniture demand currently cannot find this store at all.

Architecture

Twenty overlapping collections become one shopping path

Today the same sofa lives in Salon, Ensembles de Salon and Canapés, and four collections are effectively empty. The new structure has one canonical home per product, real facets instead of collections, and the trust pages the store currently doesn't have.

Every retired URL — /collections/chanbre, the emoji handle, the SKU product slugs — needs a 301 to its replacement on launch day. That is the single most common way a furniture site redesign loses a year of ranking.

Page blueprints

Three pages carry the whole store

Accueil
Home
  • One bar, not three. Logo, nav, search, phone, cart — the phone strip and promo banner fold into it.
  • Hero: three rooms pinned as you scroll, each naming a room, its live piece count and a link into it. Product visible above the fold on a phone.
  • Shop by room — four large tiles, real photos, piece counts.
  • En stock à Montréal — a live row of what is actually in the warehouse. This is the homepage's engine.
  • The store — storefront photo, hours, map, “Réservez un essai”.
  • Reviews — three real ones with names, linked to Google.
  • Financing — with an actual destination.
Listes de produits
Category listing
  • Facets in a left rail, sticky on desktop, a sheet on mobile — price, monthly budget, colour, material, seats, and rentre chez moi.
  • Every card carries: real name, price, monthly price, delivery chip, and a swatch row when colours exist.
  • Sort by “livrable le plus tôt” as a first-class option.
  • A short, human intro paragraph — the current SEO wall of text moves to the bottom and gets cut in half.
  • Empty and near-empty collections retired rather than shown.
Fiche produit
Product page
  • Real name and real URL — /fr/salon/sectionnel-verdun-boucle-gris, not /products/if-9085. SKU stays as a spec line.
  • 5+ photos — room shot, front, back, corner, fabric macro, folded-out.
  • Above the fold: price, instalment options, stock status, fit check, add to cart.
  • Dimensions as a labelled diagram, not a paragraph.
  • “Complétez la pièce” bundle builder.
  • Reviews, delivery terms and return policy inline — not a link away.
  • Product + Offer + AggregateRating schema on every one.

Design system

Marquise

Named for the glass-and-steel canopy over Plaza Saint-Hubert — the thing that makes their street a room. The colour is their own: #BB3703 is the rust sampled straight out of the MCS logo mark, and #171717 is its lettering. The site currently runs three different reds at once — the logo's rust, a #C62828 theme primary, and a #B30000 announcement bar. Marquise keeps the mark's rust, because the logo is the most permanent asset the business owns, and retires the other two.

What makes it read expensive is not the palette but the restraint: the rust appears perhaps three times on a page — a hairline before a section label, one italic word in the headline, the underline on a link. Everything else is ink, paper and photography. The green is the site's other voice, and it means one thing only: this one is already in the building.

Being found

The local search plan

A single-location furniture store doesn't beat Wayfair on “sectional sofa”. It beats everyone on “sofa lit Villeray”, “magasin de meubles ouvert dimanche Montréal”, and “furniture store near me” — searches with a buyer at the end of them.

Fix first

  • Meta descriptions and Open Graph on every template
  • Product + Offer + AggregateRating + LocalBusiness + Breadcrumb schema
  • Real product titles and slugs; SKU demoted to a spec
  • /fr/ ↔ /en/ with reciprocal hreflang
  • 301s for every retired URL, mapped before launch
  • Policy pages written — Google and Shopify both want them

Then build

  • Neighbourhood delivery pages: Villeray, Rosemont, Plateau, Ahuntsic, Laval, Longueuil — real content about stairs and parking, not doorway spam
  • Buying guides answering the fit-check question in long form
  • Google Business Profile: products, photos, hours, Q&A, weekly posts
  • Review requests at delivery — the 80th review is worth more than any ad
  • Point the 13,823 Facebook followers at the site, and fix the social links so the site points back

Measure honestly

Furniture e-commerce converts in roughly the 1.2–1.6% band. For a store this size the numbers that matter are narrower and more useful:

  • Fit-check completions → add-to-cart rate
  • Showroom visits booked online
  • Calls and WhatsApps from product pages
  • Financing applications started
  • Failed or refused deliveries — the fit check should drive this toward zero, and it's real money

The build

Four phases, cheapest wins first

Phase 1 is a week of work on the existing store and can ship before the redesign is even approved. Nothing in it is wasted — every fix carries into the new site.

01
Stop the bleeding
~1 week · existing site

Fix the three dead footer links. Repoint the social icons at their own accounts. Write and publish the four policy pages. Add meta descriptions and Open Graph. Build the financing page the homepage already promises. Fix “Chanbre”, the emoji URL and “TEMPS LIMITÉs”. Put the 5.0★ and the address, hours and map on the contact page.

02
Fix the data
~2–3 weeks

Rename all 171 products in human language, in both languages, with 301s from the SKU slugs. Rebuild variant options as Couleur / Configuration / Taille. Tag properly so faceted filtering becomes possible. Pull dimensions, disassembly and largest-piece width out of the descriptions into structured fields — this is the data the fit check runs on. Reshoot the 23 single-image products and the hero.

03
Build Marquise
~5–7 weeks

New theme on the design system above. Bilingual with hreflang. New IA and facets. Product card with stock status and instalment pricing. Klarna, Afterpay and Affirm, gated on price. Fit check on every large item. Bundle builder. Showroom page with booking. Reviews everywhere. Full schema. Redirect map executed on launch day.

04
Feed it
ongoing

Neighbourhood pages and buying guides. Google Business Profile worked weekly. Review capture at delivery. Expand the catalog beyond 171 — the store's floor holds far more than the site shows, and every product added is a page that can rank.

One open question worth settling early: whether the online catalog stays at 171 products or grows. A 2,262-SKU photographed and described product library already exists inside the group. If the supply relationship allows it to be sold under this brand, the same build supports it with no extra engineering — and it changes what this store can rank for entirely.
